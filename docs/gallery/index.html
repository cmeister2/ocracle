What produced these results?

Each card line is cropped by fixed coordinates and passed to ocracle’s neural text recognizer. The crop coordinates and printed transcription are human supplied. No card-name lookup, Oracle text replacement or language-model correction supplies the predictions.

When multiple preprocessing passes are enabled, ocracle retains the candidates and selects the one with the highest mean emitted-character score. Expand an example to inspect the actual method, candidate outputs and model identity. A higher score can still produce a worse transcription. When both checkpoints are available, the model selector compares the MTG-tuned network with its general OCR baseline on the same crops; fine-tuning can help some lines and hurt others.

The blur examples apply a Gaussian blur with σ = 2.4 source pixels, then reduce each crop to 30% of its original dimensions. They are deliberately difficult; lost image detail cannot always be recovered.

Character error rate compares observed output against a manual transcription of the printed image. Case and punctuation count.

The printed word matters

Reference text follows the pictured printing, including historical wording and italics. It does not substitute the current Oracle rules text. The title examples include the three standard frame families described by Wizards: Goudy Medieval, Matrix Bold and Beleren. Further crops cover Future Sight, colorshifted, Dracula, Amonkhet Invocation and D&D module treatments, plus small collector type. Special treatments are labeled by their printing when the precise typeface is unverified.
